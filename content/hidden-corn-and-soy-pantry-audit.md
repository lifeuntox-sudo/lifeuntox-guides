## Two crops, hiding almost everywhere

Corn and soy are two of the biggest crops in America. According to the [USDA](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/adoption-of-genetically-engineered-crops-in-the-united-states/recent-trends-in-ge-adoption), more than 90 percent of both are now grown from genetically engineered seed.

Food makers break these crops down into cheap parts. They become fillers, thickeners, sweeteners and oils. Then those parts slip into products where you would never think to look, from salad dressing to toothpaste.

The problem is not the sweet corn on your plate in summer. The problem is the industrial version, split into dozens of ingredients with names that never say corn or soy. That is a choice the industry made, and it leaves you doing detective work in the aisle.

This guide hands you the fix in two parts. First, a decoder: the real names corn and soy hide behind. Second, an audit: a swap sheet with a clean, whole-food choice for every common offender.

:::warn The "Contains: soy" line does not cover the oil
The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) lists soy as a major allergen, but it exempts highly refined oils from that rule. So a bottle of soybean oil can skip the "Contains: soy" line. Never rely on that line alone. Read the full ingredient list, and look for the word soybean.
:::

## Part 1: The hidden names decoder

You cannot avoid what you cannot see, so this decoder comes first. Food makers rarely print corn or soy on the front of the box. They print the name of the part instead. Here are the 28 worth learning, grouped so they are easy to recall in the aisle.

### Corn, hiding in plain sight

- **Sweeteners:** high fructose corn syrup, corn syrup, dextrose, glucose syrup and crystalline fructose. All of them are sugars made from [starch](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-184/subpart-B/section-184.1866), and in America that starch is usually corn.
- **Starches and thickeners:** corn starch, modified food starch, maltodextrin and dextrin. Under federal rules, [maltodextrin](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-184/subpart-B/section-184.1444) can be made from corn, potato or rice starch, and corn is the cheapest.
- **Acids and boosters:** citric acid, ascorbic acid (vitamin C), lactic acid and monosodium glutamate (MSG). These are made by feeding sugar to microbes. A [2018 report in Toxicology Reports](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6097542/) says nearly all factory-made citric acid starts as glucose from corn.
- **Sugar alcohols:** sorbitol, mannitol and maltitol. A sugar alcohol is a sweetener made by changing a sugar, and [sorbitol](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-184/subpart-B/section-184.1835) is made from glucose. These turn up in toothpaste and sugar-free gum.
- **Easy to miss:** xanthan gum and caramel color. Xanthan gum is grown by feeding sugar to bacteria. [Caramel color](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-73/subpart-A/section-73.85) is made by heating sugars such as dextrose and starch syrups.

### Soy, hiding in plain sight

- **Oils:** soybean oil, and any oil labeled as vegetable oil. Federal rules say a [vegetable oil](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-A/section-101.4) must name its oils in brackets, so read the brackets. The words "contains one or more of" mean the recipe can change from batch to batch.
- **Proteins:** soy protein isolate, textured vegetable protein and hydrolyzed soy protein. A hydrolyzed protein is a protein broken into small pieces to add flavor, and the [FDA](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-102/subpart-A/section-102.22) requires the label to name its source.
- **Emulsifiers:** soy lecithin and mono and diglycerides. An emulsifier keeps oil and water mixed, and these are often made from soybean oil.
- **Vitamins and extras:** mixed tocopherols, which are sold as vitamin E and are often [taken from vegetable oils](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-184/subpart-B/section-184.1890) like soy. Vegetable glycerin can come from soybean oil too.

One more name belongs on both lists: natural flavors. Under [federal rules](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-B/section-101.22), a natural flavor can come from almost any plant, and the label does not have to say which one.

## Part 2: The room-by-room swap sheet

Now put the decoder to work. These tables cover the places where corn and soy hide most, with a clean, whole-food swap for each one. Start where you shop most often, because small changes to weekly buys add up the fastest.

You do not need to throw anything out today. Replace each product as you finish it. A steady audit you complete beats a panic clear-out you give up on by the weekend.

### Pantry and baking

:::table
| Product | Where corn or soy hides | Clean swap |
|---|---|---|
| Cooking oil | Soybean oil, sold as vegetable oil | Extra virgin olive, avocado or coconut oil |
| Baking powder | Corn starch as a filler | A corn-free powder made with potato or arrowroot starch, or cream of tartar with baking soda |
| Powdered sugar | Corn starch to stop clumping | Organic powdered sugar made with tapioca starch |
| Boxed broth and stock | Maltodextrin and natural flavors | Homemade bone broth, or a brand with a short ingredient list |
| Jam and jelly | Pectin mixed with dextrose | A whole-fruit spread, or a quick chia jam |
| Chocolate chips | Soy lecithin | Chips made with cocoa butter and sunflower lecithin |
:::

### Condiments, sauces and snacks

:::table
| Product | Where corn or soy hides | Clean swap |
|---|---|---|
| Salad dressing | A soybean oil base | An olive oil dressing, or whisk your own |
| Mayonnaise | A soybean oil base | Avocado oil mayonnaise |
| Soy sauce | Soy, plus wheat and additives | Coconut aminos, a salty sauce made from coconut sap |
| Ketchup | High fructose corn syrup | No-sugar-added or honey-sweetened ketchup |
| Tortilla and corn chips | Corn, often fried in corn or soybean oil | Chips cooked in avocado or coconut oil |
:::

### Household and personal care

Corn and soy do not stop at the kitchen door. They show up across the bathroom cabinet too, and this is the step almost everyone forgets.

:::table
| Product | Where corn or soy hides | Clean swap |
|---|---|---|
| Toothpaste | Sorbitol and xanthan gum | A toothpaste built on calcium carbonate, with no sugar alcohols |
| Multivitamins | Ascorbic acid made from corn sugar | A whole-food vitamin with a short ingredient list |
| Vitamin E | Tocopherols taken from soybean oil | Vitamin E made from sunflower oil |
| Lotions and balms | Soybean oil and soy tocopherol | Simple balms made from shea butter, coconut or olive oil |
| Protein and snack bars | Soy protein isolate and natural flavors | Bars made from nuts, seeds and dates |
:::

:::warn The three words that hide the most
Watch for **vegetable oil**, **natural flavors** and **modified food starch**. Vegetable oil is very often soybean oil, so read the brackets. Natural flavors can come from corn or soy, and modified food starch is often corn unless the label names another source. Treat all three as corn or soy until the brand tells you otherwise.
:::

There is one more label that will not save you. The [USDA bioengineered food rule](https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/be) skips refined ingredients when no modified genetic material can be detected, and refining often removes it. So corn syrup and soybean oil can skip the "bioengineered" label. If you want to avoid genetically engineered crops, look for the [USDA Organic](https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/Can%20GMOs%20be%20Used.pdf) seal, which does not allow them.

## Part 3: Run your audit in one pass

:::protocol
You do not need a free weekend for this. Work through your home in the order below. You clear out the biggest sources first, and the smaller ones fall into place over the next few weeks.

1. **Start with oils and condiments.** Soybean oil is the most common hidden soy in any kitchen. Replace your cooking oil, salad dressing and mayonnaise first for the fastest win.
2. **Move to baking and pantry staples.** Check your baking powder, powdered sugar, broth and sauces against the decoder. Swap each one as it runs out.
3. **Finish in the bathroom cabinet.** Turn over your toothpaste, vitamins and lotions. This is the step most people never think to take.
4. **Build your short list.** Write down the clean brands you find and trust. Future shops then take seconds, not an hour of label reading.
:::

Tick these off as you go. Print this page and keep it on the fridge door.

- Swapped vegetable and soybean oil for olive, avocado or coconut oil
- Swapped salad dressing and mayonnaise for olive or avocado oil versions
- Checked baking powder and powdered sugar for corn starch
- Traded soy sauce for coconut aminos
- Checked broth, ketchup and sauces for corn syrup, maltodextrin and natural flavors
- Replaced toothpaste, vitamins and lotions with corn-free and soy-free versions
- Started a short list of clean brands you trust

Each clean swap you keep is one less label you ever have to read again. Within a month, your regular shop quietly takes care of itself. Start with the bottle of oil next to your stove tonight.
