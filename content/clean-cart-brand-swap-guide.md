## Good marketing is not the same as good food

You know the best food comes from a local farm. You also know you cannot get there every week. So you end up at Costco, Trader Joe's or the store on your way home, staring at twelve cartons that all promise the same thing.

That confusion is not an accident. Words like "natural," "cage-free" and "no hormones" are chosen because they sell. Some of them mean something. Many of them describe the bare legal minimum, dressed up to look like a step above.

The good news is that real standards exist, and some mainstream brands meet them. They sit on the same shelves as the marketing, often a few inches away. You just need to know which names to reach for.

This guide does the sorting for you, one aisle at a time. Each aisle names the brands worth buying, the ones that are mostly packaging, and the one label line that settles it.

![Two shopping baskets side by side. Just marketing: white eggs in a foam carton, a tub of spread, pale chicken in a foam tray and bright pink bacon. Worth buying: brown eggs, golden butter and meat wrapped in butcher paper.](assets/guides/clean-cart-brand-swap-guide-cart-1.jpg)

:::warn Four words that sell the package, not the food
**Cage-free**, **natural**, **no hormones** on chicken and **grass-fed** on its own all sound like upgrades. Each one can describe the legal minimum. Look past them for a stamp from an outside certifier, like Certified Humane or USDA Organic, or for an exact claim like "100% grass-fed and grass-finished."
:::

## Aisle 1: Eggs

Eggs carry more marketing per square inch than almost anything in the store, so they are the best place to start. The [USDA](https://www.ams.usda.gov/publications/qa-shell-eggs) defines cage-free as hens that can move around inside a barn. Free-range adds access to the outdoors, and USDA has no definition for pasture-raised at all.

**Just marketing:** a carton such as Eggland's Best Cage Free. Out of cages is better than in them, but under the USDA definition, cage-free hens can spend their whole lives indoors.

**Worth buying:** [Vital Farms Pasture-Raised](https://vitalfarms.com/products/eggs/pasture-raised-eggs/), where every hen gets at least 108 square feet of rotated pasture, audited each year to the Certified Humane standard. [Pete and Gerry's](https://certifiedhumane.org/pete-and-gerrys-eggs/), the first egg company in the country to earn Certified Humane. At Trader Joe's, the [Pasture Raised Large Brown Eggs](https://www.traderjoes.com/home/products/pdp/pasture-raised-large-brown-eggs-062124) come from hens on open pastureland.

![Just marketing: white eggs in a foam carton and a pale yellow yolk. Worth buying: brown eggs in a pulp carton and a deep orange yolk.](assets/guides/clean-cart-brand-swap-guide-eggs-1.jpg)

**At Costco:** read the Kirkland Signature carton before it goes in the cart. Look for the words "pasture-raised" next to a certifier's stamp, not just "organic" or "free-range."

**The label line:** "Pasture-raised" with a [Certified Humane stamp](https://certifiedhumane.org/range-requirements/), which means at least 2.5 acres for every 1,000 hens, or 108 square feet each.

## Aisle 2: Butter

Butter is one of the simplest foods in the store, so anything that pretends to be butter deserves a second look. By [federal law](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/321a), butter has to be made from milk or cream and contain at least 80 percent milkfat.

**Just marketing:** [Country Crock Plant Butter with Avocado Oil](https://www.countrycrock.com/en-us/our-products/plant-butter-cream/avocado-sticks). The front leads with avocado, but the label lists a blend of palm fruit, palm kernel, avocado and canola oils, plus water, pea protein and natural flavor.

**Worth buying:** [Kerrygold](https://www.kerrygoldusa.com/products/salted-butter/), made from the milk of grass-fed cows, with two ingredients: pasteurized cream and salt. It is stocked at most big grocers and warehouse stores. Any butter whose label reads only cream, or cream and salt, passes this aisle.

![Just marketing: an open tub of pale spread. Worth buying: a block of golden butter on a wooden board with a slice of bread.](assets/guides/clean-cart-brand-swap-guide-butter-1.jpg)

**The label line:** "Cream" and "salt," and nothing else.

## Aisle 3: Chicken

Chicken packaging is full of promises that the law already guarantees. The [USDA](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/meat-and-poultry-labeling-terms) does not allow hormones in raising poultry, so a "no hormones" claim on chicken has to carry the line "Federal regulations prohibit the use of hormones."

**Just marketing:** [Tyson](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/food_label_pdf/2021-07/Recall023-2021Labels.pdf) chicken that leads with "no added hormones or steroids." It is true, and it is true of every chicken sold in the country.

**Worth buying:** [Bell & Evans](https://www.bellandevans.com), which air-chills its birds, meaning they are cooled with cold air instead of a shared bath of chilled water. Mary's Free Range air-chills too. Our [Clean Meat Directory](/guide-clean-meat-directory) covers ten air-chilled brands and where to buy each one.

![Just marketing: pale wet chicken breasts in a foam tray with liquid. Worth buying: a whole chicken with dry golden skin on butcher paper.](assets/guides/clean-cart-brand-swap-guide-chicken-1.jpg)

**The label line:** "Air-chilled" on the front of the pack.

## Aisle 4: Beef

Beef labels changed more in the last ten years than any other aisle. USDA [withdrew its grass-fed standard](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2016/01/12/2016-00440/withdrawal-of-united-states-standards-for-livestock-and-meat-marketing-claims) in 2016, so "grass-fed" alone means whatever the company says. The animal can still finish on corn and grain in a feedlot.

**Just marketing:** any pack that says "grass-fed" without "grass-finished," or leans on "natural," which only means no artificial ingredients and minimal processing.

**Worth buying:** [Verde Farms](https://verdefarms.com/category/ground-beef), which sells organic beef that is 100 percent grass-fed and grass-finished. [Force of Nature](https://forceofnature.com/products/regenerative-ground-beef) sells 100 percent grass-fed and grass-finished ground beef from regenerative farms. Our [Safe Beef Buyer's Guide](/guide-safe-beef-buyers-guide) explains every beef label and stamp.

![Just marketing: bright pink ground beef in a foam tray. Worth buying: deep red grass-fed ground beef and a steak on butcher paper.](assets/guides/clean-cart-brand-swap-guide-beef-1.jpg)

**The label line:** "100% grass-fed and grass-finished." Since January 1, 2026, "Product of USA" on new packs also means the animal was [born, raised and processed here](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/18/2024-05479/voluntary-labeling-of-fsis-regulated-products-with-us-origin-claims).

## Aisle 5: Bacon

Bacon is where the curing method hides in small print. [Oscar Mayer Naturally Hardwood Smoked Bacon](https://shop.gelsons.com/store/gelsons/products/27809578-oscar-mayer-sliced-bacon-16-oz) is cured with water, salt, sugar, sodium phosphate, sodium ascorbate and sodium nitrite, a preservative that keeps meat pink.

**Worth buying:** [Applegate Naturals Uncured Bacon](https://www.hy-vee.com/aisles-online/p/2370427/applegate-naturals-hickory-smoked-uncured-thick-cut-bacon), made with no chemical nitrites or nitrates. Applegate also sells a no-sugar version for anyone cutting added sugar.

Here is the honest part. "Uncured" bacon is cured with celery powder, a natural source of nitrite, and the [label must say so](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-9/chapter-III/subchapter-A/part-317/subpart-A/section-317.17). The gain is a shorter list with no added sodium nitrite or phosphate, not a nitrite-free strip.

![Just marketing: thin glossy bright pink bacon on a plastic tray. Worth buying: thick-cut bacon with natural color on butcher paper.](assets/guides/clean-cart-brand-swap-guide-bacon-1.jpg)

**The label line:** "No nitrates or nitrites added except those naturally occurring in celery powder."

## Your next shop, aisle by aisle

:::protocol
You do not have to switch every brand this week. Change the aisles you buy from most, and the rest follows.

1. **Start with eggs.** They are the easiest win. Find the pasture-raised carton with a Certified Humane stamp.
2. **Swap the spread for butter.** Pick a butter whose label reads cream and salt only.
3. **Check the chicken for "air-chilled."** If your store has none, ask the meat counter which brands they can order.
4. **Read the beef label for "grass-finished."** "Grass-fed" alone is not enough.
5. **Flip the bacon.** Skip the packs cured with sodium nitrite and phosphate.
:::

Every brand you switch is one less label to second-guess next week. Start with the egg carton on your next trip, and let the rest of the cart follow.
