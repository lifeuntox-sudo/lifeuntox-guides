## The products you trust most

There is a special kind of betrayal in learning that the products you use every day are working against you. The soap you lather on your children. The toothpaste you swallow traces of each morning. The body wash you trust without a second thought.

These are not fringe products from questionable brands. They are the household names on every supermarket shelf, marketed as safe, gentle and family-friendly. The ingredient list tells a different story.

The exposure adds up. Personal care products are used several times a day, on skin, in the mouth and under the arms, where ingredients [pass into the body](https://www.niehs.nih.gov/health/topics/agents/endocrine) far more easily than through the skin on your arm.

Some of those ingredients act like hormones in the body. The National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences calls them endocrine disruptors, and phthalates and parabens are on its list.

The good news is that you hold more power than you think. Once you know what to look for and what to reach for instead, cleaning up your bathroom is one of the simplest health upgrades you can make. Every swap below is one you can buy today.

:::warn The one word that hides a hundred
If a label lists the single word "fragrance" or "parfum", treat it as a red flag. Under US rules a company [does not have to name](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/fragrances-cosmetics) the ingredients inside a fragrance, because the blend counts as a trade secret. That one word can hide dozens of chemicals, including phthalates. Clean brands name every scent ingredient, or use none.
:::

## Part 1: Soap, body wash and hand care

This is where most families go through the highest volume, which makes it the best place to start. The swaps here deliver the biggest drop in daily chemical exposure for the least effort.

- **Hand soap.** Avoid: triclosan, synthetic fragrance, parabens. The FDA [banned triclosan](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/antibacterial-soap-you-can-skip-it-use-plain-soap-and-water) from consumer hand washes in 2016 because makers could not show it was safe or better than plain soap. Swap: [Dr. Bronner's Pure-Castile Liquid Soap](https://www.drbronner.com/collections/pure-castile-liquid-soap).
- **Body wash.** Avoid: sodium laureth sulfate, phthalates, dyes. Sodium laureth sulfate can carry traces of [1,4-dioxane](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/potential-contaminants-cosmetics/14-dioxane-cosmetics-manufacturing-byproduct), a manufacturing by-product the FDA monitors. Swap: [Dr. Bronner's Pure-Castile Bar Soap](https://www.drbronner.com/collections/pure-castile-bar-soap).
- **Bar soap.** Avoid: synthetic fragrance, colourants. Swap: [Dr. Squatch Natural Bar Soap](https://drsquatch.com/).
- **Hand sanitiser.** Avoid: triclosan, synthetic fragrance, drying alcohols with added scent. Swap: [Dr. Bronner's Organic Hand Sanitizer](https://www.drbronner.com/collections/all).
- **Foaming face wash.** Avoid: sodium lauryl sulfate, PEG compounds, synthetic fragrance. Swap: [Attitude Super Leaves Cleanser](https://attitudeliving.com/).

Every swap in this guide passes the same three checks: no undisclosed fragrance, no ingredient on the list beside it, and a full ingredient list on the brand's own site.

Two notes on honesty. The FDA has [asked sunscreen makers for more safety data](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/understanding-over-counter-medicines/sunscreen-how-help-protect-your-skin-sun) on oxybenzone and octinoxate, while zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are already recognised as safe, which is why the swap above is a mineral one. Ingredients applied under the arms or inside the mouth face very little of the skin barrier that protects the rest of the body. That is why deodorant, toothpaste and mouthwash are the most important products to get right first.

## How to swap without wasting a bottle

:::protocol
You do not need to replace everything at once. Follow this order and your whole bathroom turns over within a couple of months, with nothing thrown away half-full.

1. **Read the label for the one word.** "Fragrance" or "parfum" with no list of what is in it means the product goes on the swap list. So does any ingredient from the avoid column beside it.
2. **Swap the three that stay on your body longest first.** Deodorant, toothpaste and lotion sit on or in you all day. Replace those as soon as the current ones run out.
3. **Then swap by volume.** Hand soap, body wash and shampoo are the products you go through fastest, so the next bottle you buy is the swap.
4. **Check the brand's full list before you reorder.** Formulas change. A clean brand publishes every ingredient on its own site; if the list is missing, choose one that shows it.
:::
