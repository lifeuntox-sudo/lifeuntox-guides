## The truth about what is in your chocolate

Chocolate should be one of life's simplest pleasures: cocoa, a little sweetener, and nothing else. Yet the chocolate aisle has quietly become a minefield. Heavy metals, lab-made additives, and beans picked by children.

The most talked-about problem is heavy metals. [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/health/food-safety/lead-and-cadmium-in-dark-chocolate-a8480295550/) tested 28 dark chocolate bars in 2022 and found lead or cadmium above California's safety levels in 23 of them. The nonprofit [As You Sow](https://www.asyousow.org/environmental-health/toxic-enforcement/toxic-chocolate) has been finding the same for a decade.

Cadmium comes up through the soil, above all in volcanic regions. Lead lands on the beans as they dry outdoors. The FDA's view is that [no level of lead is safe](https://www.fda.gov/food/environmental-contaminants-food/lead-food-and-foodwares) for children. The higher the cacao percentage, the more of both metals the bar carries.

Organic does not mean clean. A [2024 study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1366231/full) of 72 cocoa products found that organic bars were more likely to exceed the lead and cadmium limits than conventional ones, because the metals come from soil and processing, not from pesticides.

The real gold standard is a brand that tests and tells you. That is why this list exists: every brand below earns its place through published testing, ethical sourcing or clean ingredients, and the five checks at the end let you judge any bar yourself.

:::warn The percentage trap
A higher cacao percentage means more cocoa solids, and the metals live in the cocoa solids. An 85 percent bar from an untested brand carries more lead and cadmium than a 70 percent bar from one that tests. Before you buy any dark bar, look for the brand's published heavy-metal results; if there are none, choose the lower percentage and treat it as an occasional square, not a daily habit.
:::

## Part 1: The transparency-tested standouts

These five brands lead the list for one reason: they test for heavy metals and they are honest about the results. When a company publishes its lab data, or blends beans specifically to lower contamination, it has earned your trust.

1. **[Taza Chocolate](https://www.tazachocolate.com).** Stone-ground, USDA Organic and Direct Trade. The organic brand Consumer Reports singled out for lower lead and cadmium, thanks to blending beans from several origins.
2. **[Raaka Chocolate](https://www.raakachocolate.com/pages/heavy-metals-testing).** USDA Organic, non-GMO and made from unroasted beans. Publishes its third-party heavy-metal results and commits to staying under California's limits.
3. **[Pascha Chocolate](https://www.paschachocolate.com).** Free from the eight major allergens, with in-house heavy-metal testing. A consistent top pick for purity.
4. **[Mast](https://mastmarket.com).** Single-origin sourcing and organic dark bars that rank among the cleanest for both lead and cadmium in independent tests.
5. **[Spring & Mulberry](https://springandmulberry.com).** Sweetened with dates instead of refined sugar. USDA Organic, Fair Trade and a Certified B Corp.

Any brand can print the word "natural" on a wrapper. Very few will publish their actual test results or explain how they source beans to keep metals low. If you only remember five names from this guide, make it these.

## Part 2: The most ethical bean-to-bar makers

Clean chocolate is about more than what ends up in your body. It is also about the hands that grew the cocoa. About [1.6 million children](https://www.norc.org/research/projects/assessing-child-labor-in-west-africa-cocoa-farming.html) work in cocoa farming in Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire, most of them in hazardous conditions. These eight makers use Direct Trade or fair trade and pay farmers well above the market rate, which funds safer farms and cleaner drying and growing practices.

6. **[Alter Eco](https://www.alterecofoods.com).** Certified B Corp, Fair Trade, non-GMO and organic. Uses coconut oil rather than palm kernel oil, with compostable packaging.
7. **[Askinosie Chocolate](https://askinosie.com).** A bean-to-bar maker in Missouri on a Direct Trade model, with a full transparency report you can read yourself.
8. **[Divine Chocolate](https://www.divinechocolate.com).** Co-owned by the cocoa farmers who grow the beans, and a certified B Corp.
9. **[Beyond Good](https://beyondgood.com).** Single-origin chocolate made in Madagascar, close to where the cocoa grows, so more of the value stays with the farmers.
10. **[Dandelion Chocolate](https://www.dandelionchocolate.com).** Small-batch bars with two ingredients, cocoa beans and cane sugar, and deep sourcing transparency.
11. **[Equal Exchange](https://equalexchange.coop).** A worker-owned cooperative built on fair trade, with organic dark bars sourced from small farmer co-ops.
12. **[Tony's Chocolonely](https://tonyschocolonely.com).** Built on a mission to end child labour in cocoa, with fully traceable beans.
13. **[Fortunato Chocolate](https://fortunatochocolate.com).** Made from the rare Pure Nacional bean rediscovered in Peru. Single-origin and minimally processed.

Direct Trade goes further than a fair trade label. The maker deals with the farmer in person, pays well above market price, and can trace every bean to its source. That traceability keeps both exploitation and contamination out of your bar.

## How to read any chocolate label

:::protocol
New brands appear all the time. These five checks let you judge any bar you pick up, anywhere, in under a minute.

1. **Count the ingredients.** The cleanest bars contain cocoa, cocoa butter and one natural sweetener. A long list, or names you cannot pronounce, goes back on the shelf.
2. **Look for published testing.** The best brands share third-party heavy-metal results on their websites. A company proud of its numbers makes them easy to find.
3. **Check the fat.** Real chocolate uses cocoa butter. Skip bars padded with palm kernel oil, soybean oil or vague vegetable oils.
4. **Favour Ecuador and Peru.** Cocoa from these regions generally carries less cadmium than West African beans, so a single-origin South American bar is often the safer bet.
5. **Skip the lab-made sweeteners.** High fructose corn syrup, artificial flavours and synthetic additives have no place in a good bar. Dates, coconut sugar or cane sugar are all it needs.
:::

Even the cleanest dark chocolate is best in moderation. A square or two of a low-metal, ethically sourced bar gives you the flavour and the antioxidants without pushing your total intake of lead and cadmium higher. The FDA's [Closer to Zero](https://www.fda.gov/food/environmental-contaminants-food/closer-zero-reducing-childhood-exposure-contaminants-foods) plan is built on the same idea: small, steady reductions add up.
