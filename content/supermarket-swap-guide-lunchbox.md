## The lunchbox aisle was not built for your kids

Walk down the snack aisle and every box talks to children. Cartoon faces, bright colors and the words "made with real fruit" are there for a reason. They sell.

The results show up on the plate. A [2021 study in JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34374722/) found that ultra-processed foods made up 67 percent of the calories eaten by American kids and teens in 2017 to 2018, up from 61.4 percent in 1999. Ultra-processed means a food built in a factory from refined parts, like syrups, starches, oils and additives, rather than from whole food.

None of this is your fault. You have a job, a schedule and about four minutes in each aisle. Nobody has time to research every box, and the packaging is designed so you never feel you need to.

So this guide does the research for you. Each swap below sits in a standard grocery store, often in the same aisle as the box you buy now. Print it, take it with you, and make one change on your next shop.

:::warn Four label words that give a product away
Look for **juice drink**, **cheese product**, a color with a number (like **Red 40**) and **high fructose corn syrup** near the top of the list. Each one means the box is more factory than food. Flip the box before it goes in the cart. If you see one of the four, find the swap below.
:::

## The 8 swaps

Each swap shows the staple to skip, the real-food pick to buy instead, and the one label line that proves the difference.

### 1. Chocolate sandwich cookies

These are the treat kids ask for by name, so they are the easiest place to start. The [Oreo ingredient list](https://www.kroger.com/p/oreo-chocolate-sandwich-cookies/0004400004103) includes high fructose corn syrup, a cheap sweetener made from corn starch, and artificial flavor.

![Not that: chocolate sandwich cookies with white creme spilling from a plastic sleeve. Buy this: medjool dates, squares of dark chocolate and two simple sandwich cookies.](assets/guides/supermarket-swap-guide-lunchbox-cookies-1.jpg)

**Buy this:** an organic sandwich cookie such as [Newman's Own Newman-O's](https://newmansown.com/product/chocolate-creme-newman-os/), made with organic flour and organic sugar and no high fructose corn syrup. Or skip the packet: two dates and a square of dark chocolate.

**The label line:** high fructose corn syrup and "artificial flavor" are both missing.

### 2. Juice pouches and juice drinks

Drinks are where hidden sugar piles up fastest. The [American Heart Association](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27550974/) advises less than 25 grams of added sugar a day for children aged 2 to 18, and no more than one 8-ounce sugary drink a week.

Before you buy any pouch, including Kapo or Del Valle, find the percent juice line. [Federal rules](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-B/section-101.30) require it on any drink that names or pictures fruit. Anything under 100 percent is juice plus something else, usually water and sugar.

![Not that: two foil drink pouches and a glass of bright red-orange fruit drink. Buy this: a stainless water bottle, a halved orange and a glass of water with an orange slice.](assets/guides/supermarket-swap-guide-lunchbox-drinks-1.jpg)

**Buy this:** a reusable bottle of water and a piece of whole fruit. The [American Academy of Pediatrics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28562300/) says whole fruit beats juice. If you pack juice, choose 100 percent juice and keep it small: up to 4 ounces for ages 1 to 3, 4 to 6 ounces for ages 4 to 6, and 8 ounces for ages 7 and up.

**The label line:** "100% juice" on the information panel, or no juice at all.

### 3. Processed cheese slices

Cheese should be one of the simplest foods in the lunchbox. [Kraft Singles](https://www.kraftheinz.com/kraft-singles/faq) are sold as a "pasteurized prepared cheese product." Alongside cheddar, the list adds milk protein concentrate, whey, sodium phosphate and modified food starch.

![Not that: a stack of individually wrapped orange processed cheese slices. Buy this: a block of natural cheddar on a wooden board with hand-cut slices.](assets/guides/supermarket-swap-guide-lunchbox-cheese-1.jpg)

**Buy this:** a block of real cheddar and a knife. [Tillamook Medium Cheddar](https://www.kingsoopers.com/p/tillamook-medium-cheddar-sliced-cheese-farmstyle/0007283000801) lists four ingredients: cultured milk, salt, enzymes and annatto, a natural color from a seed. Slice a block on Sunday and it covers the week.

**The label line:** the word "cheese" with no "product" or "food" after it.

### 4. Ready-made lunch kits

The kits look like a full lunch in one tray. In 2024, [Consumer Reports tested 12 lunch and snack kits](https://www.consumerreports.org/health/lunch-and-snack-packs/should-you-pack-lunchables-for-your-kids-school-lunch-a1165583878/) and found lead, cadmium or both in every one, and most were high in sodium. Months later, [Kraft Heinz stopped making Lunchables](https://www.consumerreports.org/health/lunch-and-snack-packs/lunchables-pulled-from-national-school-lunch-program-a5910914129/) for the National School Lunch Program.

**Buy this:** a reusable bento box and four simple parts. Pack one protein, one fruit or vegetable, one crunchy thing and one small treat. Leftover chicken, apple slices, crackers and cheese cubes take two minutes.

**The label line:** there is no label, because you built it.

### 5. Neon orange cheese chips

Bright orange is a color someone chose. The [Doritos Nacho Cheese label](https://www.washk12.org/child-nutrition/wp-content/uploads/sites/21/2023/04/chips.pdf) lists Yellow 6, Yellow 5 and Red 40. These are petroleum-based dyes that the [FDA has asked the food industry to phase out](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/hhs-fda-phase-out-petroleum-based-synthetic-dyes-nations-food-supply).

![Not that: tortilla chips coated in neon orange powder and a pile of bright gummy fruit snacks. Buy this: plain tortilla chips, popcorn, freeze-dried strawberries and apple chips.](assets/guides/supermarket-swap-guide-lunchbox-snacks-1.jpg)

**Buy this:** plain tortilla chips made from corn, oil and salt, or a bag of popcorn with three ingredients or fewer.

**The label line:** no color followed by a number.

### 6. Gummy fruit snacks

The name promises fruit. Turn the pouch over and many brands list corn syrup and sugar near the top, and some add dyes like Red 40 and Blue 1 for the bright shapes.

**Buy this:** fruit you can see. Freeze-dried strawberries or apple chips with one ingredient have the same crunch and chew. Fruit bars made only from fruit, such as apple and strawberry, work too.

**The label line:** fruit is the first ingredient, and there are no colors with numbers.

### 7. Flavored yogurt tubes

Yogurt has a healthy image, and flavored tubes ride on it. The [Nutrition Facts label](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/added-sugars-nutrition-facts-label) now shows "Added Sugars" on its own line, so the sugar in each tube is easy to spot.

**Buy this:** plain whole-milk yogurt in a small reusable pot, with berries stirred in at home. A drizzle of honey adds sweetness for children over one year old. Never give honey to a baby under one.

**The label line:** "Added Sugars 0g" on the plain tub.

### 8. Cheese crackers

Crackers fill a lot of lunchboxes, and most are refined white flour and oil with a dusting of flavor. They fill the gap without feeding much.

**Buy this:** crackers made from nuts and seeds, such as [Simple Mills Almond Flour Crackers](https://simplemills.com/Products/Product/Fine-Ground-Sea-Salt-Almond-Flour-Crackers.aspx), built on a flour of almonds, sunflower seeds and flax seeds. Or pair whole-grain crackers with your sliced block cheddar.

**The label line:** a whole food, like a nut, seed or whole grain, as the first ingredient.

## Your Buy This, Not That table

Print this and keep it on the fridge door or in your phone. Every swap below sits in a standard grocery store, and the unit price on the shelf tag tells you which one costs less per ounce.

| Sandwich cookies | Cookies with high fructose corn syrup and artificial flavor | Organic sandwich cookies, or dates and dark chocolate | No high fructose corn syrup |

| Crackers | Refined-flour cheese crackers | Nut and seed crackers | A whole food first |
:::

## Your 10-minute Sunday lunchbox reset

:::protocol
You do not need to change everything this week. Start with one swap, and the rest fall into place over a month.

1. **Pick one swap.** Choose the item your kids eat most and buy the swap on your next shop.
2. **Prep on Sunday.** Slice a block of cheese, wash the fruit and cut the vegetables. It takes about ten minutes.
3. **Fill the bottles.** Line up reusable water bottles in the fridge so the pouch is never the easy choice.
4. **Use the four-part formula.** One protein, one fruit or vegetable, one crunchy thing and one small treat in every box.
5. **Let your kids choose.** Give them two clean options for the treat. Kids eat what they pick.
:::

Every box you swap is one less label to argue with. Start with the item your kids eat most, and make that one change on your next grocery run.
