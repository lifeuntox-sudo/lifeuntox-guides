## Your first cup deserves a second look

Coffee is the one thing most of us buy every single week, so it is worth getting right. For many people it is the very first thing that touches their body each day.

The bag tells you the roast, the origin and a few tasting notes. It does not tell you three things that matter more.

The first is mold. Coffee beans can carry ochratoxin A, a toxin made by mold, which the World Health Organization's cancer agency lists as a [possible human carcinogen](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11355758/). Europe caps it at [3 micrograms per kilo](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/915/oj/eng) of roasted coffee. The United States has no limit for it in coffee at all.

The second is farm chemicals. Only certified organic coffee is grown under [USDA rules](https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/organic/national-list) that ban synthetic pesticides unless they are on a short approved list. The third is how decaf loses its caffeine. The FDA still allows [methylene chloride](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-173/subpart-C/section-173.255), a chemical solvent, and the label does not have to say so.

This list does the checking for you. It names the brands that pass, from a cheap store bag to a lab-tested premium roast, so you know exactly what to buy tomorrow morning.

![Just marketing: a metal can of dull pre-ground coffee with a plastic scoop. Worth buying: glossy whole beans spilling from a kraft bag beside a cup of black coffee.](assets/guides/clean-coffee-safe-swap-list-shelf-1.jpg)

:::warn "Naturally decaffeinated" does not tell you how
Many decaf bags say "naturally decaffeinated" or nothing at all. That leaves out the method. Look for the exact words **Swiss Water Process**, **mountain water process** or **CO2 process** on the bag. If a decaf names no method, assume a solvent was used and pick another bag.
:::

## The decaf aisle: where the solvent shows up

Decaf is where the label leaves out the most, so it deserves its own check. The FDA allows decaf to keep up to [10 parts per million](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-173/subpart-C/section-173.255) of methylene chloride. That is the same chemical the EPA [banned for most uses](https://www.epa.gov/assessing-and-managing-chemicals-under-tsca/risk-management-methylene-chloride) in paint strippers and other products in 2024.

In December 2023, health groups asked the FDA to remove it from food. That [petition](https://www.hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/fdcc/index.cfm?id=FAP_4A4839&set=FAP-CAP) is still listed as under review, so decaf makers can still use it.

The nonprofit Clean Label Project [tested popular decafs](https://cleanlabelproject.org/wp-content/uploads/CLP-Decaf-Coffee-White-Paper.pdf) and found methylene chloride in these: Kirkland Signature Decaf Dark Roast, Maxwell House Decaf Original Roast, Great Value Decaf Classic Roast, Gevalia Decaf House Blend, Café Bustelo Decaf, Seattle's Best Decaf Portside Blend and Kroger Decaf Classic.

Every result was under the FDA limit, and brands can change their supplier. The point is simple: none of those bags told you. In the same tests, Kicking Horse Decaf and Allegro Decaf Organic French Roast came back clean.

![Solvent decaf: roasted beans beside a lab beaker of clear liquid and a dropper. Water-processed decaf: roasted and green beans beside a glass carafe of water.](assets/guides/clean-coffee-safe-swap-list-decaf-1.jpg)

The fix is one line on the bag. [Swiss Water Process](https://www.swisswater.com/pages/coffee-decaffeination-process) uses only water, temperature and time, with carbon filters to catch the caffeine, and the result is [99.9% caffeine-free](https://www.swisswater.com/blogs/sw/from-origin-to-decaf-how-swiss-water-process-protects-integrity-coffee-beans).

## Your clean coffee plan for tomorrow morning

:::protocol
You do not have to change your whole routine. Check the bag you already own, then make one swap.

1. **Flip your current bag.** Look for a USDA Organic seal. No seal means no organic check on farm chemicals.
2. **Read the decaf line.** If you drink decaf, find the words Swiss Water, mountain water or CO2. No method named means pick another bag.
3. **Pick your tier.** Want lab testing? Start with Purity, Lifeboost, Kion or Danger Coffee. On a budget? Equal Exchange or the Whole Foods Market Organic bag.
4. **Buy whole beans.** Grind them just before you brew, so you can see and smell what you are getting.
5. **Store them dry.** Keep beans in an airtight, opaque container away from heat and damp, as Purity advises, because mold needs moisture to grow.
:::
