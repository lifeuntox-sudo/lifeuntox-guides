## You deserve more than a reassuring letter

Every year your local water authority sends a report declaring your tap water safe. It arrives with official letterhead and confident language, and most families file it away without a second glance.

That trust is understandable, and it is also a mistake. The word "safe" in those reports means legal limits, not biological ones. A contaminant can sit just under the legal line and still build up in your body over years of daily drinking.

There is a bigger gap the report rarely mentions. Utilities test at the treatment plant and in the mains, not at your kitchen tap. Everything between the plant and your glass, including your own pipes, is invisible to that letter.

Lead is the clearest example. The EPA states there is [no known safe level of lead](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/basic-information-about-lead-drinking-water) in a child's blood, and lead enters water from household plumbing long after the supply has been tested and cleared.

The only way to know what is in your glass is to test at the tap. This guide gives you the tools: how to test independently, which filters really work, and how to read the warning signs your home is already showing you.

:::warn A test strip cannot see PFAS
PFAS, the "forever chemicals", sit in water at parts per trillion. No strip or home meter can measure them. A US Geological Survey study found PFAS in [at least 45 percent of the nation's tap water](https://www.usgs.gov/news/national-news-release/tap-water-study-detects-pfas-forever-chemicals-across-us). The only reliable test is a certified lab using EPA Method 533 or 537.1. Any product claiming to detect PFAS with a colour strip is misleading you.
:::

## Part 1: Test your water independently

The single most powerful step you can take is to test your own water at your own tap. It replaces guesswork with hard numbers about what your family drinks, independent of any official assurance.

**Use a certified mail-in lab, not a strip.** The cheap dip strips in hardware stores give vague colour readings that miss the contaminants that matter most. A certified lab analyses a sample from your tap and returns a report measured against real health guidelines. Every state keeps a list of [certified drinking water laboratories](https://www.epa.gov/dwlabcert/contact-information-certification-programs-and-certified-laboratories-drinking-water), and several mail-in services use them.

**Test for the four that matter:**

- **Lead.** The top priority, above all in homes built before 1986, when [lead pipes and lead solder](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/basic-information-about-lead-drinking-water) were still allowed.
- **PFAS.** The forever chemicals now found in nearly half of US tap water. The EPA set the first [national limits for PFAS](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas) in 2024, and utilities have until 2029 to meet them.
- **Chlorine and chloramine.** The disinfectants that keep water safe in the mains, and that many families prefer to filter out at the tap.
- **Nitrates.** Vital on well water and in farming areas. The federal limit is [10 milligrams per litre](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/national-primary-drinking-water-regulations), and infants are the most at risk.

**When to test.** Test once to set a baseline, then once a year, or any time the taste, colour or smell changes. An older home or recent plumbing work makes testing more urgent, not less.

**Three certified mail-in kits we recommend:**

**A free option worth checking first.** Several states, including New York, Colorado and New Hampshire, offer free PFAS testing for private well owners. Call your state health department and ask for the private well program.

## Part 2: Choose a filter that works

Once you know what is in your water, the next step is removing it. This is where most families waste money. The popular pitcher on every counter was designed to improve taste, not to remove the contaminants you care about.

**The pitcher trap.** A standard pitcher improves the taste of your water by reducing chlorine, and that is where its power ends. If your test showed lead or PFAS, you need a filter certified to remove them, not a taste filter.

**How to check a filter's claim.** Look for NSF/ANSI numbers on the box. NSF 53 covers lead reduction, NSF 58 covers reverse osmosis, and PFAS claims are tested under 53 or 58. Before you trust any box, check the [NSF certified product listings](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/DWTU/listings_leadreduction.asp). No number, no trust.

## Part 3: The warning signs of aging pipes

Your home gives you clues about its plumbing every day. Reading them makes you your own first line of defence, and catches problems long before a lab report or a symptom does.

- **Discoloured water.** Brown or yellow tints, above all first thing in the morning, point to rust and corrosion inside old iron or galvanised pipes.
- **A metallic taste.** A distinct metal flavour usually means metals are dissolving out of your plumbing.
- **Frequent repairs.** A pattern of leaks and patches means the pipes are near the end of their life.
- **A home built before 1986.** It can still have lead pipes or lead solder at the joints.
- **Stains around fixtures.** Blue-green stains mean copper corrosion; rust-coloured stains mean iron.

**Know your service line.** The most useful fact about your home is whether the pipe from the street is lead. Since late 2024 every water utility has had to publish a [lead service line inventory](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/lead-service-line-replacement), and most now offer an address lookup. You have every right to that answer.

**The morning flush rule.** Water that sits in pipes overnight has the longest contact with lead and other metals. If your plumbing is old, [run the cold tap](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/protect-your-tap-quick-check-lead) for 30 seconds to 2 minutes each morning before drinking or cooking, until a certified filter is in place. Never use hot tap water for drinking, cooking or baby formula; heat pulls more lead from pipes.

## Your water protection checklist

:::protocol
Save this page or print it. Working through the steps in order gives your family independent, verified protection that no annual letter can offer.

1. **Order a certified mail-in test** and collect the sample from the kitchen tap, first draw in the morning.
2. **Test for lead, PFAS, chlorine and nitrates** at minimum. Add bacteria and arsenic on well water.
3. **Look up your home's build year** and check the utility's inventory for a lead service line.
4. **Match a certified filter** to the contaminants your test found, and confirm the NSF number on the box.
5. **Run the cold tap each morning** until the filter is installed, and never cook with hot tap water.
6. **Watch for the five signs**: discoloured water, metallic taste, repeat repairs, pre-1986 plumbing, stained fixtures.
7. **Replace cartridges on the maker's schedule.** A spent filter stops removing lead and PFAS without any change in taste.
8. **Read the annual report properly.** Your utility must send a [Consumer Confidence Report](https://www.epa.gov/ccr) each July; look for any contaminant listed above its limit.
9. **Retest once a year**, or sooner if the taste, colour or smell changes.
:::
